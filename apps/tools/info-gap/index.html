How to Use & Classroom Guide

Build a Student A / Student B speaking activity in 4 steps

What is an info gap?

Two students read the same text, but each copy has different words missing. Student A has what B is missing and vice versa, so they must talk — asking and answering real questions — to complete their sheets. It is a classic, low-prep speaking activity for any level.

Building it

  1. Passage & Title: paste your text and give the activity a title and instructions (both print on the sheets).
  2. Blanks: pick a brush, then click or click-and-drag across words in the Interactive Editor. Pink = missing on A's sheet, Blue = missing on B's sheet. Or set a density and press Smart Auto-Split, then fine-tune. Swap A↔B flips every blank; Undo (Ctrl+Z) reverts mistakes.
  3. Question Helper: press Auto-Draft Questions to turn each sentence with a missing word into a prompt. Edit freely — e.g. turn "Anna works in a ______." into Where does Anna work? The (Ask about: …) part only prints on the Teacher Key.
  4. Page Setup & Print: check the Student A, Student B, Side-by-Side and Teacher Key tabs, then print.

Running it in class

  1. Put students in pairs. Give one the Student A sheet and the other the Student B sheet.
  2. They sit face to face (or back to back for a challenge) and must not look at each other's sheet.
  3. Students take turns asking about their numbered blanks: "What does Anna's mum make on Sundays?" The partner reads the answer from their sheet.
  4. When both are finished, they compare sheets — or check with the Teacher Key.

Tips

  • Blank meaningful words (names, places, numbers, times) — they produce natural Wh- questions.
  • Keep A and B roughly balanced so both students speak equally.
  • Allow spelling questions: "How do you spell that?" is great extra practice.
  • Everything auto-saves in this browser; use Save As to keep a library of activities.
